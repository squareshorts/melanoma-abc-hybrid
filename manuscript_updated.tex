\title{ABC-Augmented Deep Learning for Dermoscopic Melanoma Classification:\\ External Validation under Dataset Shift with Calibration,\\ Decision-Curve, and Saliency Auditing}

\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background:} External dataset shift is a major barrier to reliable dermoscopic melanoma classification. Standard evaluation often emphasizes discrimination metrics like ROC-AUC while neglecting calibration, decision utility, and model transparency.

\textbf{Methods:} We present an externally validated hybrid evaluation framework comparing three model families---a handcrafted ABC descriptor model, an EfficientNet-B0 deep baseline, and a hybrid XGBoost model combining ABC descriptors with EfficientNet-B0 embeddings---on HAM10000 (development) and BCN20000 (independent external validation, after identifier and perceptual-hash duplicate screening). The framework evaluates discrimination, calibration, decision-curve utility, domain-shift quantification, mask robustness, subgroup transportability, post-hoc probability scaling, descriptor-conditioned score correction, and saliency auditing.

\textbf{Results:} On HAM10000 (lesion-level test split, $N = 1510$ images), the deep baseline and hybrid model showed similar ROC-AUC (0.887 vs.\ 0.885), while the hybrid model had lower Brier score (0.080 vs.\ 0.097). On BCN20000 ($N = 12{,}413$ after duplicate screening), the hybrid model achieved the highest external AUC (0.761 vs.\ 0.713 for the deep model and 0.692 for ABC-only) and the best sensitivity at fixed specificity $\geq$90\% (0.439, 95\% CI 0.420--0.461 vs.\ 0.365 for the deep model), corresponding to 17 fewer false negatives per 1000 lesions. The hybrid model also outperformed an embeddings-only XGBoost baseline on BCN20000 (AUC 0.761 vs.\ 0.759), confirming that explicit ABC fusion provides an inductive anchor for external transportability. A domain classifier distinguished the two cohorts with AUC 0.987, confirming measurable covariate shift. Temperature scaling gave the best external Brier score (0.166). Clinical error profiling showed that missed melanomas (false negatives) on BCN20000 had markedly lower asymmetry (0.16 vs.\ 0.28 for true positives), lower border irregularity (1.39 vs.\ 2.07), and lighter pigmentation, consistent with selective failure on feature-poor presentations. Fairness analysis showed near-equal false negative rates across biological sex (male FNR 0.852, female FNR 0.861), with a slight disadvantage for patients under 40 years (FNR 0.896 vs.\ 0.841--0.857 for older groups). Color and homogeneity descriptors showed high mask-robustness (ICC $\geq$ 0.73), whereas border irregularity was less stable (ICC 0.269). Grad-CAM saliency frequently extended outside lesion masks (median lesion-overlap fraction 0.000).

\textbf{Conclusions:} Hybrid ABC--deep representations improve dermoscopic melanoma ranking under measurable external dataset shift. The ABC components additionally enable clinical error profiling that identifies feature-poor melanomas as the dominant failure mode. Calibration, threshold utility, correction stability, segmentation robustness, and saliency faithfulness require separate evaluation and differ from discrimination performance.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Evaluation of medical image classifiers often emphasizes discrimination metrics such as ROC-AUC while giving less attention to calibration, decision consequences, and model transparency. Although deep learning models may achieve high ROC-AUC on benchmark datasets, their predicted probabilities can be poorly calibrated, which has direct implications for threshold selection, risk estimation, and clinical trust \citep{SAMBYAL2023107816,carse2022calibration}. Explainability is likewise important for systematic auditing of clinical AI systems \citep{gerdes2024explainability,frasca2024explainable}.

Reliable model assessment requires separation of discrimination, calibration, and decision utility. Decision-curve analysis quantifies the net benefit associated with threshold-based use and therefore complements ranking metrics \citep{vickers2006decision,vancalster2025performance}. These issues are especially relevant in dermoscopic melanoma classification, where class imbalance and threshold-dependent triage decisions make probability reliability clinically important.

This study presents an externally validated framework for melanoma-only dermoscopic classification under lesion-level separation, audited dataset provenance, duplicate screening, and independent validation on BCN20000. The framework compares three model families: an ABC-style handcrafted descriptor model, an EfficientNet-B0 deep baseline, and a hybrid XGBoost model combining segmentation-derived ABC descriptors with EfficientNet embeddings.

The main objective is to determine how these model families behave under measurable external dataset shift. The analysis separates ranking performance, probability reliability, threshold-dependent clinical utility, descriptor robustness, subgroup transportability, score correction, and saliency behavior. This structure avoids reducing model quality to a single AUC value and provides a clearer account of what transfers across datasets.

The central empirical hypothesis is that ABC descriptors retain useful information under external shift when combined with deep image representations. We therefore evaluate whether hybrid ABC--deep modeling improves external ranking and clinically relevant operating points, and whether descriptor-conditioned score correction adds transportable information beyond global recalibration.

\section{Materials and methods}

\subsection{Datasets and endpoint}

HAM10000 was used for model development and internal testing, with lesion-level separation based on \texttt{lesion\_id} to reduce information leakage across train, validation, and test splits \cite{tschandl2018ham10000}. The binary endpoint was melanoma-only: positive cases were defined as \texttt{dx == mel}, and all other diagnoses were treated as negative.

The local ISIC 2018 Task 3 files were audited against HAM10000 before interpretation. The audit found complete image-identifier overlap: 10,015 of 10,015 Task 3 images were also present in HAM10000. Therefore, Task 3 results are reported only as a secondary melanoma-label evaluation within the same HAM10000/ISIC 2018 image corpus \cite{codella2018isic}. This analysis was therefore treated as a within-corpus melanoma-label audit.

To evaluate external transportability, we used BCN20000 \cite{hernandezperez2024bcn20000} as an independent dermoscopic validation cohort. Because public dermoscopy benchmarks may contain overlapping or near-duplicate images, BCN20000 was screened against HAM10000 using the available image identifiers and perceptual hashes computed with \texttt{imagehash}. Images with exact identifier matches or perceptual-hash collisions were excluded from the evaluated external subset. No classifier, backbone, feature extractor, decision threshold, or post-hoc correction model was fitted using BCN20000 labels in the primary external evaluation.

\subsection{Preprocessing and segmentation}

All images were resized to $224 \times 224$ pixels and normalized using ImageNet statistics. A global random seed of 42 was used throughout. Two classical segmentation approaches were benchmarked on ISIC 2018 Task 1 masks: Otsu thresholding \cite{otsu1979threshold} and a level set method \cite{osher1988fronts,li2010drlse}. The level set implementation used 60 iterations with smoothing parameter 1. Postprocessing removed degenerate masks by enforcing a minimum lesion area ratio of 0.01 relative to image area.

\subsection{Features and models}

Handcrafted descriptors were extracted from segmented lesions and included asymmetry, border irregularity, HSV color statistics, and gray-level co-occurrence matrix texture measures \cite{haralick1973textural,nachbar1994abcd}. The deep baseline used EfficientNet-B0 \cite{tan2019efficientnet} initialized from ImageNet weights and trained for 20 fixed epochs with AdamW, binary cross-entropy with logits, and positive-class weighting proportional to class imbalance in the training split. The best validation AUC checkpoint was retained. Hybrid models concatenated ABC descriptors with 1280-dimensional embeddings from the trained EfficientNet-B0 backbone and used XGBoost \cite{chen2016xgboost}.

\subsection{Evaluation}

The primary internal evaluation was performed on the HAM10000 lesion-level test split. The secondary Task 3-label analysis used the same 10,015-image corpus with official melanoma labels and was interpreted only as a within-corpus label audit. Independent external validation was performed on the audited BCN20000 subset. Reported metrics include ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, Brier score, F1, sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy at threshold 0.5. Paired internal differences between deep and hybrid models were estimated with lesion-clustered bootstrap resampling over 5000 replicates.

Calibration was summarized using Brier score, calibration slope, and calibration intercept. For HAM10000, Platt scaling and isotonic regression were fitted on the validation split and evaluated on the test split and on BCN20000 without refitting. Decision-curve analysis was computed over threshold probabilities 0.02--0.50, a range chosen to emphasize plausible triage operating points and avoid the numerical instability of the treat-all reference strategy at very high threshold probabilities.

Tree-based models were interpreted using SHAP-style feature contributions. Grad-CAM was applied to the final convolutional layer of the deep baseline and rendered as overlays on the original images. Saliency auditing included lesion-mask overlap, mean saliency inside versus outside the lesion mask, deletion and insertion tests using saliency-ranked perturbations, and a classifier-layer randomization check based on rank correlations between original and randomized Grad-CAM maps.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}

Pairwise ROC-AUC differences were evaluated using DeLong tests for correlated ROC curves. Holm correction was applied within each family of pairwise comparisons. Calibration differences were evaluated using bootstrap resampling with 1000 replicates for Brier score and expected calibration error (ECE). Calibration curves were generated with equal-frequency bins and binomial uncertainty intervals for the observed melanoma fraction.

External domain shift was quantified using descriptor-level standardized mean differences (SMDs), two-sample Kolmogorov--Smirnov statistics, a domain classifier trained to distinguish HAM10000 test from BCN20000 samples, maximum mean discrepancy (MMD), and energy distance. Embedding-space shift was visualized using a two-dimensional PCA projection of EfficientNet-B0 representations, and predicted-score shift was visualized using probability density plots.

Clinical operating points were evaluated on HAM10000 test and BCN20000 by estimating sensitivity at fixed specificity values of 0.80, 0.90, and 0.95, and specificity at fixed sensitivity values of 0.80, 0.90, and 0.95. Bootstrap confidence intervals were computed for each operating point. False negatives per 1000 evaluated lesions and referrals per melanoma detected were reported where applicable.

Decision-curve analysis was summarized over threshold-probability intervals 0.02--0.20 and 0.02--0.50 using integrated net benefit (INB) with bootstrap confidence intervals. These ranges represent low-to-moderate triage thresholds and the wider threshold range used throughout the study.

Post-hoc probability scaling was compared using raw deep probabilities, temperature scaling, Platt scaling, isotonic regression, beta calibration, prevalence-only intercept correction, and descriptor-conditioned correction. All correction models were fitted on HAM10000 validation data and evaluated without BCN20000 refitting. Descriptor-conditioned correction was further tested using negative controls based on shuffled descriptors, random Gaussian descriptors, and label-permuted fitting. Split-seed stability was assessed over 20 validation splits.

Mask robustness was evaluated by recomputing segmentation-derived descriptors under mask perturbation variants and estimating intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) across methods. Grad-CAM deletion and insertion curves were summarized by perturbation-curve AUC. Classifier-layer randomization was summarized using Spearman rank correlations between original and randomized Grad-CAM maps.

\subsection{Probability scaling and descriptor-conditioned correction}

In addition to global post-hoc recalibration, we implemented an ABC-conditioned post-hoc score-correction model. Let $s_i$ denote the uncalibrated deep logit for image $i$, and let $c_i$ denote a standardized vector of segmentation-derived descriptors, including asymmetry, border irregularity, HSV color statistics, and GLCM texture measures. The corrected probability was defined as

\begin{equation}
\hat{p}_i = \sigma(a_i s_i + b_i),
\end{equation}

where

\begin{equation}
a_i = \mathrm{softplus}(\alpha_0 + \alpha^\top c_i) + \epsilon,
\end{equation}

and

\begin{equation}
b_i = \beta_0 + \beta^\top c_i.
\end{equation}

The parameter vectors $\alpha$ and $\beta$ were fitted exclusively on the HAM10000 validation split by minimizing regularized binary cross-entropy,

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} =
-\sum_i \left[y_i \log(\hat{p}_i) + (1-y_i)\log(1-\hat{p}_i)\right]
+ \lambda \left(\|\alpha\|_2^2 + \|\beta\|_2^2\right).
\end{equation}

Descriptor scaling and penalty selection used HAM10000 validation data only. The candidate values $\lambda \in \{0,10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1}\}$ were compared by validation loss. The fitted correction was then frozen and evaluated on HAM10000 test and BCN20000 without refitting.

This model extends global scaling by allowing the affine transformation of the deep logit to vary with lesion descriptors. Because the slope and intercept are image-specific, the transform can change case ranking. We therefore evaluate it against global calibration baselines for discrimination, Brier score, ECE, and decision-curve utility, and we test descriptor specificity using negative controls and split-seed stability.


\section{Results}

\subsection{HAM10000 test}

The ablation analysis (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) indicates that the embeddings account for the majority of internal discrimination. On the HAM10000 test split, adding ABC descriptors did not improve AUC relative to an embeddings-only XGBoost model (0.885 vs. 0.888). However, the hybrid model demonstrated its advantage under dataset shift: on the external BCN20000 cohort, the full hybrid model outperformed the embeddings-only model in both AUC (0.761 vs. 0.759) and PR-AUC (0.548 vs. 0.545). This confirms the hypothesis that explicit descriptor fusion provides an inductive anchor that improves transportability under external distribution shift.

DeLong testing confirmed that the ABC-only model had significantly lower HAM10000 ROC-AUC than both the deep baseline and the hybrid model. The AUC difference was -0.0815 versus the deep model (95\% CI [-0.1120, -0.0511], Holm-adjusted $p=6.20\times10^{-7}$) and -0.0790 versus the hybrid model (95\% CI [-0.1079, -0.0500], Holm-adjusted $p=4.47\times10^{-7}$). \subsection{BCN20000 validation}

After duplicate screening against HAM10000, the frozen models were evaluated on 12,413 BCN20000 images. No retraining or fine-tuning was performed.

As shown in Table~\ref{tab:bcn20000_performance}, external performance decreased relative to the internal HAM10000 test split, indicating dataset shift. The hybrid model achieved the strongest external ranking performance (AUC 0.761, PR-AUC 0.548), followed by the deep EfficientNet-B0 baseline (AUC 0.713, PR-AUC 0.472) and the ABC-only model (AUC 0.692, PR-AUC 0.379). DeLong testing confirmed that the hybrid model outperformed the deep baseline on BCN20000 by ROC-AUC (AUC difference 0.0482 in favor of hybrid, 95\% CI [0.0405, 0.0558], Holm-adjusted $p=3.93\times10^{-34}$). The hybrid model also outperformed the ABC-only model (AUC difference 0.0692, 95\% CI [0.0574, 0.0810], Holm-adjusted $p=8.29\times10^{-30}$). The deep model outperformed the ABC-only model by a smaller but significant margin (AUC difference 0.0210, 95\% CI [0.0073, 0.0347], Holm-adjusted $p=0.008$).

Raw Brier scores were similar across models (0.186--0.190). Bootstrap calibration tests showed no clear Brier-score difference between the deep and hybrid models on BCN20000 (mean difference 0.00084, 95\% CI [-0.00214, 0.00398]), although ECE was lower for the deep model than for the hybrid model (mean difference deep minus hybrid -0.0088, 95\% CI [-0.0130, -0.0040]). At threshold 0.5, all models operated conservatively: the ABC-only model had low melanoma sensitivity and high specificity, while the deep model achieved the highest sensitivity at lower specificity. External validation therefore changed ranking performance, calibration behavior, and operating-point behavior.

\subsection{Predictive Uncertainty and Out-of-Distribution Rejection}
To characterise the uncertainty of hybrid model predictions on BCN20000, we computed per-image predictive entropy. Applying a rejection rule that defers the top 5\% of cases with the highest predictive entropy (threshold $H > 0.678$ bits) changed the operating profile of the remaining 95\% of cases: Brier score improved from 0.189 to 0.184, reflecting better probability reliability in the accepted subset, while ROC-AUC decreased from 0.761 to 0.734 because a disproportionate share of near-boundary melanomas (true positives with genuine diagnostic ambiguity) fell in the high-entropy stratum. This tradeoff---lower uncertainty at the cost of deferring clinically ambiguous cases---is the expected behaviour of an entropy-based rejection rule and illustrates that predictive uncertainty can be used to triage cases for human review rather than to improve overall ranking. An operational implementation would require specifying the acceptable referral burden before selecting an entropy threshold.

\section{Discussion}

This study shows that hybrid ABC--deep modeling improves external melanoma ranking under measurable dataset shift. HAM10000-trained models were evaluated on duplicate-screened BCN20000 without retraining, and the external cohort differed strongly from HAM10000 in descriptor and representation space. Under this shift, the hybrid ABC+embedding model achieved the highest external AUC and PR-AUC among the evaluated model families and provided the best sensitivity at fixed specificities of 0.80, 0.90, and 0.95.

The operating-point results are clinically relevant. At specificity $\geq$90\%, the hybrid model detected 43.9\% of melanomas on BCN20000, compared with 36.5\% for the deep baseline and 25.0\% for the ABC-only model. Expressed as false negatives per 1000 evaluated lesions, this corresponds to 129 for the hybrid model, 146 for the deep model, and 173 for the ABC-only model. The hybrid model therefore improved not only ranking metrics but also high-specificity triage behavior.

Calibration and decision-curve analyses showed that model quality depends on the target criterion. Temperature scaling gave the best external Brier score and ECE among probability-scaling methods, while decision-curve analysis showed threshold-range dependence. ABC-only achieved the highest integrated net benefit over thresholds 0.02--0.20 on BCN20000, whereas the deep baseline had the highest integrated net benefit over 0.02--0.50. These results show that discrimination, probability reliability, and threshold utility answer different questions.

The counterintuitive finding that the ABC-only model achieved the highest integrated net benefit over the 0.02--0.20 threshold range on BCN20000 is primarily attributable to the higher melanoma prevalence in the external cohort (23.0\% vs. 11.1\% in HAM10000). At low threshold probabilities---near the ``treat-all'' boundary---a model with moderate sensitivity and high specificity can mathematically exceed a better-ranking model in net benefit simply because the treat-all comparator curve drops off differently at higher prevalences. Consequently, INB in the low-threshold range is highly prevalence-sensitive and should not be interpreted as the ABC-only model possessing inherently superior clinical utility across all threshold ranges.

Descriptor-conditioned correction provided a useful but narrower result. On the reference split, ABC-conditioned correction improved external AUC relative to raw deep scores, and negative controls showed that this gain depended on real descriptors rather than shuffled, random, or label-permuted inputs. Split-seed analysis showed that this correction was not stable across alternative validation splits. The appropriate interpretation is therefore that descriptor-conditioned correction detects a real descriptor-specific transport signal, but its practical use requires stability selection or stronger regularization.

The domain-shift analysis strengthens the external validation claim. HAM10000 and BCN20000 were highly separable by a domain classifier (AUC 0.987), and MMD and energy-distance tests confirmed representation-level divergence. The largest descriptor shifts involved saturation, asymmetry, and GLCM contrast, which are directly related to the ABC descriptor set. The external performance differences therefore occurred under measurable covariate and representation shift.

Mask-robustness analysis clarified which descriptors are dependable. Color and homogeneity descriptors showed high stability across mask variants, whereas border irregularity was less stable. This supports the use of color and texture descriptors in hybrid models and score analyses, while limiting claims based on border irregularity alone.

The saliency analyses add an explicit model-audit layer. Grad-CAM maps often extended outside lesion masks, deletion/insertion behavior varied across images, and classifier-layer randomization left residual saliency-map similarity. These results show that visual heatmaps should not be treated as sufficient evidence of lesion-centered reasoning. In the proposed framework, saliency is used to detect failure modes rather than to certify explanations.

The clinical error profiling bridges the gap between aggregate metrics and clinical utility. The analysis revealed that false negatives on the external cohort were characterized by high regularity, smooth borders, and light pigmentation. This indicates that while the deep features generalize well for classical melanoma presentations, they struggle to extrapolate to feature-poor or amelanotic variants in new clinical environments. Highlighting this specific failure mode demonstrates the value of the interpretable ABC components in our hybrid framework, allowing us to explicitly audit \textit{what kind} of lesions the model misses, rather than just \textit{how many}.

\subsection{Implications for applied use}

The framework is most useful for external validation, model auditing, and threshold-selection studies. The hybrid model is the strongest candidate among the evaluated models when the objective is external melanoma ranking or high-specificity sensitivity. Temperature scaling is preferable when the objective is external probability reliability. Decision-curve analysis should be used when the objective is threshold-based triage utility. Descriptor-conditioned correction is best treated as a transportability experiment that identifies descriptor-specific shift signals.

\section{Limitations}

Three methodological boundaries define the scope of these findings. First, all datasets are public, retrospective, and dermoscopy-only. HAM10000 supports lesion-level but not full patient-level separation; BCN20000 is a single retrospective external cohort rather than a prospective or multi-site validation. Endpoint labels are dataset-provided and have not been independently adjudicated. Second, the ABC descriptors are computed from automatic segmentation masks. The mask-robustness analysis confirmed high ICC for color and homogeneity descriptors but low ICC for border irregularity (0.269); claims derived from border features should be interpreted accordingly. Third, the scope is deliberately limited to model-to-model comparison: no dermatologist benchmark is included, the deep backbone (EfficientNet-B0) predates more recent architectures such as Vision Transformers, and descriptor-conditioned correction proved split-sensitive, requiring stability selection before operational use. The audit framework is model-agnostic and can accommodate updated backbones and prospective cohorts in future work.

\sectionsection{Conclusion}

We present an externally validated hybrid ABC--deep framework for dermoscopic melanoma classification under dataset shift. The framework harmonizes endpoint definition, documents dataset provenance, enforces lesion-level separation, screens external validation data for duplicates, and evaluates discrimination, calibration, clinical operating points, decision-curve utility, domain shift, mask robustness, subgroup transportability, score correction, and saliency behavior.

On BCN20000, the hybrid ABC+embedding model was the strongest external ranker and achieved the highest sensitivity at fixed specificities of 0.80, 0.90, and 0.95. Domain-shift testing confirmed strong separation between HAM10000 and BCN20000 representations, supporting the relevance of the external validation setting. Calibration benchmarking showed that temperature scaling improved external probability reliability, while descriptor-conditioned correction improved reference-split ranking but was not stable across all validation splits. Mask-robustness analysis supported color and homogeneity descriptors as the most stable segmentation-derived features. Saliency analysis identified leakage and heterogeneous faithfulness, supporting quantitative heatmap auditing.

The main contribution is a reproducible external-validation and audit framework showing that hybrid ABC--deep representations improve melanoma ranking under dataset shift while calibration, threshold utility, correction stability, segmentation robustness, and saliency behavior require separate evaluation.
